At today's NEAR Intents volume of $125.8M/day, protocol buybacks offset ~20 cents of every dollar of new token issuance. HSP-029, approved by governance on November 2, 2026 with ratification steps still pending, sets a 24-month ramp from 2.5% to 1.6% annual issuance, which lifts that ceiling to ~30 cents roughly two years after the ramp starts (base case: mid 2029). Neither path reaches break-even at flat volume. The critical condition: Intents volume must reach $409-639M/day, 3.3-5.1× its current level, depending on which issuance regime and capture rate scenario prevails.

1. Background and Mechanism

NEAR Intents is the protocol's intent-based cross-chain execution layer, deployed at intents.near (and its earlier form, defuse-alpha.near). Users submit signed intents (requests to swap, bridge, or transfer assets across chains) that competing solvers fill. The protocol charges solvers a fee on matched volume; a portion of that fee flows to the treasury as protocol revenue, which the NEAR Foundation uses to buy back NEAR on the open market.

NEAR's token model allows validators to mint new NEAR up to an annual issuance cap, currently 2.5% of total supply. On a 1.310B-token total supply at $3.58, this produces roughly 89,700 NEAR ($322,000) of new supply every day. Buybacks must exceed this rate for the token to be net deflationary; anything below 100% offset means the treasury is still adding to supply on net.

2. The Break-Even Formula

The model has three variables:

Core Identity

Break-even volume ($/day) = (NEAR issued/day × NEAR price) ÷ capture rate

B = (S × cap / 365 × P) / r
where S = supply, cap = annual issuance cap, P = NEAR price, r = fee capture rate

At 5.03 basis points capture rate and the current 2.5% cap: break-even requires $639M/day, 5.1× today's Intents volume. Cutting the cap to 1.6% (HSP-029 floor) reduces that threshold to $409M/day (3.3×).

How Often Has Revenue Actually Covered Issuance?

A claim often repeated in NEAR commentary is that revenue already exceeds issuance "on volatile days." The exact count, from 557 days of history with matched price data: daily protocol revenue covered the full 2.5%-cap issuance on 6 days (1.1%), and would have covered the 1.6%-floor issuance on 8 days. The best single day, January 15, 2026, covered 2.5%-cap issuance 2.2 times over. Break-even days exist, but they are rare spikes, not a trend; the structural gap is the subject of this memo.

Revenue Reconciliation: Two Sources, One Right Answer

Two data sources report different cumulative revenue figures for NEAR Intents. NEAR's public revenue dashboard (revenue.near.org), which tallies the buyback wallet, shows $16.71M in total inflows; DefiLlama reports $8.71M in protocol revenue. This 1.92x gap is not a data error. It reflects what each source is measuring.

The revenue.near.org figure is a gross wallet count: it tallies every token entering the buyback address, including solver settlements that transit during execution and are subsequently rebated back out. The DefiLlama figure is the net protocol revenue: the portion that permanently accrues to the protocol after those rebates. The difference (~$8M) is solver rebate flow: value that moved through the wallet but did not stay.

This model uses the DefiLlama net figure throughout. It is the correct input for the 5.03 bps capture rate (revenue divided by volume) and for all offset and break-even calculations. A model built on the gross revenue.near.org figure would overstate the effective capture rate by nearly 2x and produce a falsely optimistic break-even threshold.

That such a basic quantity depends on which definition of revenue one adopts reinforces this memo's central finding: the capture mechanism is off-contract policy rather than enforced protocol, and that is the strongest argument for moving the take on-chain.

On-Chain Evidence: What the Settlement Contract Shows

Decoding raw dip4 settlement events from intents.near (more than 50,000 fills across more than 20,000 settlements, a contiguous window beginning Oct 28, 2026, frozen Nov 13, 2026) reveals something the aggregate data cannot: the settlement contract itself collects an explicit fee of roughly 0.01 bps per fill side, several hundred times below the 5.03 bps revenue rate implied by DefiLlama. Protocol revenue is therefore generated off the settlement contract, through application-layer fees or solver arrangements. The capture rate this model depends on is policy, not protocol: nothing at the contract level guarantees it, and it can change in either direction without governance. This is the strongest argument for the fee switch discussed in Section 8: moving the take into the contract converts a soft revenue arrangement into an enforced one.

The same events quantify the concentration risk flagged in Section 7. The top three solver accounts fill 66% of settled volume (Herfindahl index 0.21, 38 active solver accounts). Concentration this high cuts both ways: a solver exit is a material volume event, and solvers hold real bargaining power against any attempt to raise the protocol take. One caveat on the method: the solver side of a settlement is identified as whichever account has the higher global fill count, so concentration is measured over accounts defined partly by that same count, which biases the index upward.

On volume quality the honest statement is narrow. No settlement in the window has one account on both sides, which rules out the crudest form of wash trading and nothing more. Round trips among non-solver accounts run at 17% of entries, up from 8% in the first window measured, and that metric excludes solver accounts, which is precisely where inventory cycling would appear. These are screens that failed to find a problem, not evidence that none exists.

These figures come from roughly 70 chain hours beginning October 28; they are structure measurements, not long-run averages. They describe the public side of the protocol only: a confidential settlement path, quantified under Key Risks, receives more stablecoin value than public settlement does. Ingestion was stopped on November 13, 2026 so that the published dataset is byte-identical to the file behind these numbers, and every figure in this subsection regenerates from those raw events with one command.

4. HSP-029: The Governance Catalyst

HSP-029 was submitted to the House of Stake in October 2026 and passed its vote on November 2, 2026 with roughly 99.96% support. It mandates a linear reduction in the annual issuance cap from 2.5% → 1.6% over 24 months, stepping down by 0.9 percentage points evenly across the ramp. The 1.6% floor holds permanently once reached, 24 months after the ramp activates.

The Vote Is Not Activation

The proposal is explicit that it "cannot take effect on a House of Stake vote alone." Per its own implementation plan, activation occurs one full quarter after ratification, and "validator adoption is ultimately what makes it binding": each validator independently adopts the protocol upgrade that carries the new cap. The proposal names no calendar dates. Ratification occurs when the Security Council veto window closes on November 16, 2026 (no veto has been issued at the time of writing), so the earliest possible ramp start is mid February 2027 (one quarter of grace, instant validator adoption). This memo's base case assumes April 2027, allowing a realistic adoption lag, with a late case of July 2027. Under the base case the 1.6% floor arrives around April 2029, not late 2028. Much public commentary models the ramp from the vote date; that reading has no basis in the proposal text and overstates the near-term offset trajectory. The dashboard exposes the ramp start as an explicit assumption, including the as-voted date as a labeled counterfactual. The vote passed 3,287,729 veNEAR for, 0 against, across 36 votes.

What HSP-029 Changes

At the floor, daily NEAR issuance drops from 89,701 to 57,409 NEAR, a 36% reduction. The offset ratio improves from 19.7% to 30.8% at flat volume. The break-even volume requirement falls from $639M to $409M/day. Both figures assume the 5.03 bps capture rate holds; under the conservative 4.33 bps estimate (90-day trailing) the floor-scenario break-even is $475M/day.

What HSP-029 Does Not Change

The proposal does not touch the capture rate, the solver fee structure, or the protocol's ability to direct revenue to buybacks rather than operational costs. The fee-switch mechanism (activating a protocol-level take from solver fees, rather than relying on voluntary contribution) is a separate governance question and is the single lever that could most directly improve the offset ratio without requiring volume growth.

6. What Must Be True for Break-Even

Three conditions must hold simultaneously:

Volume growth of 3.3-5.1×. Intents volume needs to reach $424-663M/day. That is plausible if NEAR becomes the primary settlement layer for cross-chain DeFi volume from Ethereum, Solana, and Bitcoin, but it requires winning solver share in a competitive market where UniswapX, CoW Protocol, and 1inch are already established.

HSP-029 holding at the floor. The 24-month ramp is governance-enforced; a subsequent proposal could raise the cap again. The investment case is structurally weakened if issuance reverts upward before volume catches up.

Capture rate holding up. This is the weakest link in the chain, because the rate is not a constant and carries no error bar in the headline figures. Daily capture has a standard deviation of 2.5 bps on a mean of 3.8, and the 30-day trailing average has ranged between 1.9 and 6.0 bps over the last four months. The estimator matters too: the same 30 days give 5.03 bps as a mean of daily ratios, 4.88 bps as total revenue over total volume, and 4.53 bps as a median, while the 90-day trailing figure is 4.33 bps. Every break-even number in this memo is linear in whichever is chosen. Because the rate is set off the settlement contract rather than enforced by it, nothing holds it in place: if more of the fee revenue is absorbed by solvers through rebates or competitive pressure, capture compresses and break-even volume rises with it. Variance of this size in a parameter the whole case rests on is exactly what a contract-enforced floor would remove.

7. Key Risks

Volume concentration risk. If a small number of solvers or routes account for the majority of Intents volume, any disruption to that activity (a competing protocol, a solver exit, a bridge exploit) causes an outsized drop in buyback revenue.

Price sensitivity. The break-even formula is linear in NEAR price: a 50% NEAR price decline (to ~$2.19) requires 50% more volume to break even, all else equal. Conversely, a price rally to $8+ cuts the required volume in half.

Visibility risk: the flow is going private. Decoded transfer events show a confidential settlement path, routed through a treasury account inside intents.near, receiving roughly 57%, 63% and 71% of stablecoin value across the three chain days fully covered, counted once per settlement on both sides. That account is a routing hub rather than a sink: over the window it received $43.2M and paid out $45.5M, so it moves value rather than capturing it. Reading the same account from contract state rather than from events extends that picture to 268 consecutive days: one mt_batch_balance_of view call per UTC day at the matching archival block height shows the stablecoin float growing from zero on February 18, 2026 to $34.9M, against a median of $6.2M, and at the gross routing measured on the decoded days that float is 1.21 days of flow. The account therefore turns over rather than accumulates across the entire history, not merely across the ingested window. What state cannot show is the fee charged on that path, because a balance records where value sits, not what was taken to move it. Settlement on that path nonetheless happens outside the public event stream. Two consequences follow. First, chain-visible evidence, including this memo's own on-chain measurements, describes a shrinking slice of total protocol activity, and that boundary is stated wherever those numbers appear. Second, fee and revenue reporting for the private path cannot be independently verified from public data, which deepens the capture-is-policy finding: an investor cannot audit what they cannot see.

HSP-029 reversal or non-adoption. The ramp has not activated: ratification steps and validator adoption are still ahead, and a proposal that is never adopted by validators never binds. Even after activation, a future proposal restoring the 2.5% cap would widen the gap back to the worst-case scenario.

8. The Fee-Switch Option: The Argument Only This Data Can Make

The most structurally important upgrade not yet priced into the model is a protocol-level fee switch: a contract-enforced take on matched Intents volume, analogous to Uniswap's fee switch. Two findings from this project's own data combine into an argument for it that neither finding supports alone.

First, the current capture is policy, not protocol. Decoded settlement events show the contract itself collects roughly 0.01 bps per fill side (Section 3); the 5.03 bps the buyback actually receives is generated off-contract and can change in either direction without governance, as it did over the eleven days covered by this memo. Revenue this soft deserves a discount; a contract-enforced take removes that discount.

Second, the demand response is unknown, and that is a cost of the argument rather than support for it. The difference-in-differences analysis of the February 23, 2026 fee activation (Section 10) initially appeared to show no volume damage, but it does not survive its own parallel-trends diagnostic: NEAR's volume fell 19% across the switch, the positive estimate exists only because the controls fell further, and trend-adjusting turns it into a 29% relative decline. The event also tested a contract-level fee of 0.01 bps, so it says little about a 5 bps take. An enforced fee switch is therefore a decision under uncertainty on the demand side, and this memo does not pretend otherwise.

Together: a contract-enforced take of 5 bps on top of the current effective capture is the one lever that is simultaneously in governance's control, volume-independent, and in governance's control. At 10.03 bps total capture and a $3.58 NEAR price, break-even volume falls to roughly $205M/day at the 1.6% floor (1.6x current volume, versus 3.3x without it) and $321M/day at the current cap. The offset ratio at today's volume rises to roughly 61% at the floor. This argument requires both the on-chain fee measurement and the counterfactual; this analysis has both.

9. The Price Question

Does buyback activity move NEAR's price? This memo's break-even mechanism does not require that it does, but it is the investor's last question and deserves a direct answer. A weekly regression of NEAR returns on buyback intensity (prior-week revenue scaled by prior-week market cap, lagged to avoid reverse causality and mechanical price contamination), controlling for BTC and ETH returns with Newey-West errors, finds no detectable effect: t = 0.59, permutation p = 0.67 across 1,000 reshuffles, 38 weeks since the fee switch. The expectation of a null at this sample size was stated before running, and the result is published as found. The null is weak evidence rather than strong: with 38 weekly observations the smallest effect this test could reliably detect is about a 6.6% move in weekly return per one standard deviation of buyback intensity, against a fitted effect of 1.4% and a weekly return standard deviation of 14.6%. Buybacks average 0.0053% of market cap per week, so no realistic effect would clear that bar. The finding is better stated as a matter of scale: a flow this small cannot be a price mechanism, whatever the regression says. Two further disclosures: the robustness specification that drops the September to October rally returns t = -1.50, the largest magnitude in the set and with the sign reversed, and the volume placebo correlates 0.72 with the treatment regressor, so it is a weak placebo rather than a clean one.

The diagnostic pattern is itself informative. The contemporaneous specification shows a strong association (t = 3.5) that vanishes under the lag and flips sign when the September to October rally is excluded: the signature of price driving volume and revenue within the week, not buybacks driving price. At current scale, buybacks are a supply-side accounting flow, not a price catalyst; the thesis stands on the issuance-offset mechanism, not on a short-run price effect.

Verdict · November 13, 2026
NEAR Intents is a genuine economic engine for NEAR, but at current scale it offsets 20 cents of issuance per dollar created. HSP-029, once ratified and adopted, raises that ceiling to 30 cents over two years, meaningful progress but structurally insufficient without volume growth. The investment thesis becomes sound when two of the three following conditions are met: (1) Intents daily volume crosses $450M, (2) the fee switch activates at ≥5 bps, or (3) NEAR price sustains above $8. The fee-switch decision is the single highest-conviction near-term catalyst because it is in governance's control and volume-independent. Until then, HSP-029 is a necessary but not sufficient step toward a self-sustaining buyback loop.

10. Quantitative Model Limitations

Volume Forecast

Break-even is defined as a sustained crossing: the 30-day average volume reaching the threshold, not a one-day spike touching it. Thresholds are recomputed at every model run from the live NEAR price and the current 30-day capture rate, so they move with the market rather than freezing at a publication-date price.

  • Two model classes were tested and rejected before this one, and the rejections are recorded in the model output. GBM on daily volume double-counted the Ito correction in calibration, and its one-year dispersion (daily sigma near 0.35 compounding to 6.8 annualized) makes any crossing probability an artifact. An unconstrained AR(1) on the smoothed series fits phi near 0.998, indistinguishable from a unit root, so its implied stationary level is an unstable extrapolation.
  • The model used is a random walk with drift on the log 30-day average, residual bootstrap, no normality assumption, with a 30-day block bootstrap of daily returns as a non-parametric check.
  • Trend extrapolation is the model's honest weakness: the fitted drifts (roughly +122%/yr on the 365d window, +396%/yr on 180d) reflect genuine recent growth but cannot continue indefinitely, so long-horizon probabilities are upper bounds.
  • The two calibration windows disagree, but that spread measures estimator instability more than regime uncertainty. The 365-day drift is 0.00098/day against a standard error of 0.00113, statistically indistinguishable from zero, and simulation treats it as known. Moving the window start by a few weeks swings the annualised drift several fold.
  • Residuals are resampled independently although they are differences of a 30-day moving average and therefore mechanically autocorrelated (lag-1 autocorrelation about 0.6). This understates path dispersion by roughly two to three times, which pushes the low-end probabilities down and the high-end ones up. The reported spread is wider than the evidence warrants in both directions.
  • A random walk cannot anticipate structural breaks: an exploit, a lost integration, or saturation of the current growth driver.

Fee-Switch Difference-in-Differences

Raw ATT = 1.11× (bootstrap CI 0.97 to 1.25). That number should not be read as evidence the fee switch was harmless, and the limitations below are the reason. They are reported because the design does not support the conclusion it initially appeared to support.

  • NEAR's own volume fell 19% across the switch (post/pre ratio 0.81). The estimate is positive only because the three controls fell further.
  • Parallel trends fails. NEAR's pre-period slope was +0.00297/day (+195% annualised) against a control mean of -0.00199/day (-52% annualised). Carrying that differential across the gap between window midpoints gives a trend-adjusted ATT of 0.71×, a 29% relative decline, pointing the opposite way from the raw figure. The model output reports both.
  • The bootstrap CI resamples daily rows and therefore captures sampling noise in the means, not the real source of uncertainty. Dispersion across the three controls (log growth -0.43, -0.06, -0.46) gives a standard error of 0.13 and an interval of 0.86 to 1.42.
  • With four units the permutation test cannot return a p-value below 0.25, so it is incapable of rejecting the null at conventional levels regardless of the data.
  • External validity is limited: the event measured is the activation of a fee whose contract-level component this memo measures at 0.01 bps. Using it to argue a 5 bps enforced take is safe is an extrapolation of several hundred times.
  • Controls are larger, more mature aggregators, and the window coincided with a broad DeFi contraction.
Interpretation: the volume effect of the fee switch is not identified by this design, in either direction. The honest conclusion is that this natural experiment cannot settle the question, and the fee-switch argument in Section 8 rests on the on-chain fee measurement rather than on this result.
