At today's NEAR Intents volume of $127M/day, protocol buybacks offset ~19 cents of every dollar of new token issuance. HSP-029, approved by governance on November 2, 2026 with ratification steps still pending, sets a 24-month ramp from 2.5% to 1.6% annual issuance, which lifts that ceiling to ~30 cents roughly two years after the ramp starts (base case: mid 2029). Neither path reaches break-even at flat volume. The critical condition: Intents volume must reach $424-663M/day, 3.3-5.2× its current level, depending on which issuance regime and capture rate scenario prevails.

1. Background and Mechanism

NEAR Intents is the protocol's intent-based cross-chain execution layer, deployed at intents.near (and its earlier form, defuse-alpha.near). Users submit signed intents (requests to swap, bridge, or transfer assets across chains) that competing solvers fill. The protocol charges solvers a fee on matched volume; a portion of that fee flows to the treasury as protocol revenue, which the NEAR Foundation uses to buy back NEAR on the open market.

NEAR's token model allows validators to mint new NEAR up to an annual issuance cap, currently 2.5% of total supply. On a 1.310B-token total supply at $4.37, this produces roughly 89,700 NEAR ($392,000) of new supply every day. Buybacks must exceed this rate for the token to be net deflationary; anything below 100% offset means the treasury is still adding to supply on net.

2. The Break-Even Formula

The model has three variables:

Core Identity

Break-even volume ($/day) = (NEAR issued/day × NEAR price) ÷ capture rate

B = (S × cap / 365 × P) / r
where S = supply, cap = annual issuance cap, P = NEAR price, r = fee capture rate

At 5.91 basis points capture rate and the current 2.5% cap: break-even requires $663M/day, 5.2× today's Intents volume. Cutting the cap to 1.6% (HSP-029 floor) reduces that threshold to $424M/day (3.3×).

How Often Has Revenue Actually Covered Issuance?

A claim often repeated in NEAR commentary is that revenue already exceeds issuance "on volatile days." The exact count, from 557 days of history with matched price data: daily protocol revenue covered the full 2.5%-cap issuance on 6 days (1.1%), and would have covered the 1.6%-floor issuance on 8 days. The best single day, January 15, 2026, covered 2.5%-cap issuance 2.2 times over. Break-even days exist, but they are rare spikes, not a trend; the structural gap is the subject of this memo.

Revenue Reconciliation: Two Sources, One Right Answer

Two data sources report different cumulative revenue figures for NEAR Intents. NEAR's public revenue dashboard (revenue.near.org), which tallies the buyback wallet, shows $16.71M in total inflows; DefiLlama reports $8.71M in protocol revenue. This 1.92x gap is not a data error. It reflects what each source is measuring.

The revenue.near.org figure is a gross wallet count: it tallies every token entering the buyback address, including solver settlements that transit during execution and are subsequently rebated back out. The DefiLlama figure is the net protocol revenue: the portion that permanently accrues to the protocol after those rebates. The difference (~$8M) is solver rebate flow: value that moved through the wallet but did not stay.

This model uses the DefiLlama net figure throughout. It is the correct input for the 5.91 bps capture rate (revenue divided by volume) and for all offset and break-even calculations. A model built on the gross revenue.near.org figure would overstate the effective capture rate by nearly 2x and produce a falsely optimistic break-even threshold.

The murkiness is not unique to this analysis: independent public analyses of the same wallets disagree with each other about how much of DefiLlama's reported revenue actually reaches the buyback addresses, with published estimates ranging from roughly 69% to 97%. That such a basic quantity is contested reinforces this memo's central finding that the capture mechanism is off-contract policy rather than enforced protocol, and it is the strongest argument for moving the take on-chain.

On-Chain Evidence: What the Settlement Contract Shows

Decoding raw dip4 settlement events from intents.near (2,852 fills across 1,151 settlements, a contiguous window of Oct 28, 2026, ingestion ongoing) reveals something the aggregate data cannot: the settlement contract itself collects an explicit fee of roughly 0.01 bps per side, several hundred times below the 5.91 bps revenue rate implied by DefiLlama. Protocol revenue is therefore generated off the settlement contract, through application-layer fees or solver arrangements. The capture rate this model depends on is policy, not protocol: nothing at the contract level guarantees it, and it can change in either direction without governance. This is the strongest argument for the fee switch discussed in Section 8: moving the take into the contract converts a soft revenue arrangement into an enforced one.

The same events quantify the concentration risk flagged in Section 7. In the initial window the top three solver accounts filled 86% of settled volume (Herfindahl index 0.33, 17 active solver accounts); as ingestion coverage grows the live figure on the dashboard has eased toward three quarters, still highly concentrated. Concentration this high cuts both ways: it means a solver exit is a material volume event, and it means solvers hold real bargaining power against any attempt to raise the protocol take. Volume quality, however, screens clean: zero bilateral self-fills in the window, and non-solver round-trip activity of about 8%, consistent with organic flow rather than wash trading.

These figures come from a short window of one trading day and carry time-of-day bias; they are structure measurements, not averages. The ingestion pipeline continues to run, and every figure in this subsection regenerates from the raw events with one command as coverage grows.

4. HSP-029: The Governance Catalyst

HSP-029 was submitted to the House of Stake in October 2026 and passed its vote on November 2, 2026 with roughly 99.96% support. It mandates a linear reduction in the annual issuance cap from 2.5% → 1.6% over 24 months, stepping down by 0.9 percentage points evenly across the ramp. The 1.6% floor holds permanently once reached, 24 months after the ramp activates.

The Vote Is Not Activation

The proposal is explicit that it "cannot take effect on a House of Stake vote alone." Per its own implementation plan, activation occurs one full quarter after ratification, and "validator adoption is ultimately what makes it binding": each validator independently adopts the protocol upgrade that carries the new cap. The proposal names no calendar dates. Ratification occurs when the Security Council veto window closes on November 16, 2026 (no veto has been issued at the time of writing), so the earliest possible ramp start is mid February 2027 (one quarter of grace, instant validator adoption). This memo's base case assumes April 2027, allowing a realistic adoption lag, with a late case of July 2027. Under the base case the 1.6% floor arrives around April 2029, not late 2028. Much public commentary models the ramp from the vote date; that reading has no basis in the proposal text and overstates the near-term offset trajectory. The dashboard exposes the ramp start as an explicit assumption, including the as-voted date as a labeled counterfactual. The vote passed 3,287,729 veNEAR for, 0 against, across 36 votes.

What HSP-029 Changes

At the floor, daily NEAR issuance drops from 89,701 to 57,409 NEAR, a 36% reduction. The offset ratio improves from 19.2% to 30.0% at flat volume. The break-even volume requirement falls from $663M to $424M/day. Both figures assume the 5.91 bps capture rate holds; under the conservative 4.11 bps estimate the floor-scenario break-even is $610M/day.

What HSP-029 Does Not Change

The proposal does not touch the capture rate, the solver fee structure, or the protocol's ability to direct revenue to buybacks rather than operational costs. The fee-switch mechanism (activating a protocol-level take from solver fees, rather than relying on voluntary contribution) is a separate governance question and is the single lever that could most directly improve the offset ratio without requiring volume growth.

6. What Must Be True for Break-Even

Three conditions must hold simultaneously:

Volume growth of 3.3-5.2×. Intents volume needs to reach $424-663M/day. That is plausible if NEAR becomes the primary settlement layer for cross-chain DeFi volume from Ethereum, Solana, and Bitcoin, but it requires winning solver share in a competitive market where UniswapX, CoW Protocol, and 1inch are already established.

HSP-029 holding at the floor. The 24-month ramp is governance-enforced; a subsequent proposal could raise the cap again. The investment case is structurally weakened if issuance reverts upward before volume catches up.

Capture rate staying at or above 5.91 bps. The 4.11 bps estimate (90d trailing revenue/volume) represents the conservative case. If more of the fee revenue is absorbed by solvers through rebates or competitive pressure, the effective capture rate compresses and the break-even volume rises.

7. Key Risks

Volume concentration risk. If a small number of solvers or routes account for the majority of Intents volume, any disruption to that activity (a competing protocol, a solver exit, a bridge exploit) causes an outsized drop in buyback revenue.

Price sensitivity. The break-even formula is linear in NEAR price: a 50% NEAR price decline (to ~$2.19) requires 50% more volume to break even, all else equal. Conversely, a price rally to $8+ cuts the required volume in half.

HSP-029 reversal or non-adoption. The ramp has not activated: ratification steps and validator adoption are still ahead, and a proposal that is never adopted by validators never binds. Even after activation, a future proposal restoring the 2.5% cap would widen the gap back to the worst-case scenario.

8. The Fee-Switch Option: The Argument Only This Data Can Make

The most structurally important upgrade not yet priced into the model is a protocol-level fee switch: a contract-enforced take on matched Intents volume, analogous to Uniswap's fee switch. Two findings from this project's own data combine into an argument for it that neither finding supports alone.

First, the current capture is policy, not protocol. Decoded settlement events show the contract itself collects roughly 0.01 bps per side (Section 3); the 5.91 bps the buyback actually receives is generated off-contract and can change in either direction without governance. Revenue this soft deserves a discount; a contract-enforced take removes that discount.

Second, demand did not flinch when fees switched on. The difference-in-differences analysis of the February 23, 2026 fee activation (Section 10) finds no statistically significant volume impact relative to three aggregator controls (ATT 1.10x, CI 0.97 to 1.25). Within the power of this test, Intents demand tolerated the introduction of fees.

Together: a contract-enforced take of 5 bps on top of the current effective capture is the one lever that is simultaneously in governance's control, volume-independent, and empirically consistent with no demand damage. At 10.91 bps total capture and a $3.85 NEAR price, break-even volume falls to roughly $203M/day at the 1.6% floor (1.6x current volume, versus 2.9x without it) and $317M/day at the current cap. The offset ratio at today's volume rises to roughly 64% at the floor. No competing analysis can make this argument, because it requires both the on-chain fee measurement and the counterfactual.

9. The Price Question

Does buyback activity move NEAR's price? This memo's break-even mechanism does not require that it does, but it is the investor's last question and deserves a direct answer. A weekly regression of NEAR returns on buyback intensity (prior-week revenue scaled by prior-week market cap, lagged to avoid reverse causality and mechanical price contamination), controlling for BTC and ETH returns with Newey-West errors, finds no detectable effect: t = 0.61, permutation p = 0.62 across 1,000 reshuffles, 37 weeks since the fee switch. The expectation of a null at this sample size was stated before running, and the result is published as found.

The diagnostic pattern is itself informative. The contemporaneous specification shows a strong association (t = 4.1) that vanishes under the lag and flips sign when the September to October rally is excluded: the signature of price driving volume and revenue within the week, not buybacks driving price. Public analyses reporting a weak positive link are consistent with this reading. At current scale, buybacks are a supply-side accounting flow, not a price catalyst; the thesis stands on the issuance-offset mechanism, not on a short-run price effect.

Verdict · November 2, 2026
NEAR Intents is a genuine economic engine for NEAR, but at current scale it offsets 19 cents of issuance per dollar created. HSP-029, once ratified and adopted, raises that ceiling to 30 cents over two years, meaningful progress but structurally insufficient without volume growth. The investment thesis becomes sound when two of the three following conditions are met: (1) Intents daily volume crosses $450M, (2) the fee switch activates at ≥5 bps, or (3) NEAR price sustains above $8. The fee-switch decision is the single highest-conviction near-term catalyst because it is in governance's control and volume-independent. Until then, HSP-029 is a necessary but not sufficient step toward a self-sustaining buyback loop.

10. Quantitative Model Limitations

Volume Forecast

Break-even is defined as a sustained crossing: the 30-day average volume reaching the threshold, not a one-day spike touching it. Thresholds are recomputed at every model run from the live NEAR price and the current 30-day capture rate, so they move with the market rather than freezing at a publication-date price.

  • Two model classes were tested and rejected before this one, and the rejections are recorded in the model output. GBM on daily volume double-counted the Ito correction in calibration, and its one-year dispersion (daily sigma near 0.35 compounding to 6.8 annualized) makes any crossing probability an artifact. An unconstrained AR(1) on the smoothed series fits phi near 0.998, indistinguishable from a unit root, so its implied stationary level is an unstable extrapolation.
  • The model used is a random walk with drift on the log 30-day average, residual bootstrap, no normality assumption, with a 30-day block bootstrap of daily returns as a non-parametric check.
  • Trend extrapolation is the model's honest weakness: the fitted drifts (roughly +122%/yr on the 365d window, +396%/yr on 180d) reflect genuine recent growth but cannot continue indefinitely, so long-horizon probabilities are upper bounds.
  • The calibration windows disagree because the regime changed mid-2026; the disagreement is information about regime uncertainty, not a defect to average away.
  • A random walk cannot anticipate structural breaks: an exploit, a lost integration, or saturation of the current growth driver.

Fee-Switch Difference-in-Differences

ATT = 1.10× (95% CI: 0.97-1.25×). The confidence interval includes 1.0: no statistically significant volume impact is detected relative to the three controls.

  • Four units total (NEAR + 3 controls): the permutation distribution has only four distinct points. The 75th-percentile rank is a weak signal, not a statistically powerful one.
  • Controls (1inch, Paraswap, Jupiter) are larger, more mature aggregators with different liquidity depth and routing characteristics.
  • The 90-day window coincided with a broader DeFi volume contraction; the market cycle is not isolated from the treatment effect.
  • Pre-period slopes differ: NEAR's pre-trend was +0.003/day while controls averaged -0.002/day. This suggests the parallel trends assumption is imperfect; the DiD estimate may conflate trend with treatment.
  • Bootstrap CIs are constructed by resampling within pre/post periods independently. Within-period autocorrelation is not preserved, which may narrow the stated intervals.
Interpretation: the fee switch did not detectably harm volume relative to peers. This is consistent with either (a) no harm or (b) insufficient statistical power to detect a modest effect at this sample size.
