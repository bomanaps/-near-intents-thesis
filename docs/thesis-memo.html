1. Background and Mechanism

NEAR Intents is the protocol's intent-based cross-chain execution layer, deployed at intents.near (and its earlier form, defuse-alpha.near). Users submit signed intents (requests to swap, bridge, or transfer assets across chains) that competing solvers fill. The protocol charges solvers a fee on matched volume; a portion of that fee flows to the treasury as protocol revenue, which the NEAR Foundation uses to buy back NEAR on the open market.

NEAR's token model allows validators to mint new NEAR up to an annual issuance cap, currently 2.5% of circulating supply. On a 1.310B-token supply at $4.37, this produces roughly 89,700 NEAR ($392,000) of new supply every day. Buybacks must exceed this rate for the token to be net deflationary; anything below 100% offset means the treasury is still adding to circulating supply on net.

2. The Break-Even Formula

The model has three variables:

Core Identity

Break-even volume ($/day) = (NEAR issued/day × NEAR price) ÷ capture rate

B = (S × cap / 365 × P) / r
where S = supply, cap = annual issuance cap, P = NEAR price, r = fee capture rate

At 5.91 basis points capture rate and the current 2.5% cap: break-even requires $663M/day, 5.2× today's Intents volume. Cutting the cap to 1.6% (HSP-029 floor) reduces that threshold to $424M/day (3.3×).

Revenue Reconciliation: Two Sources, One Right Answer

Two data sources report different cumulative revenue figures for NEAR Intents. The NEAR RPC buyback wallet shows $16.71M in total inflows; DefiLlama reports $8.71M in protocol revenue. This 1.92x gap is not a data error. It reflects what each source is measuring.

The NEAR RPC figure is a gross wallet count: it tallies every token entering the buyback address, including solver settlements that transit during execution and are subsequently rebated back out. The DefiLlama figure is the net protocol revenue: the portion that permanently accrues to the protocol after those rebates. The difference (~$8M) is solver rebate flow: value that moved through the wallet but did not stay.

This model uses the DefiLlama net figure throughout. It is the correct input for the 5.91 bps capture rate (revenue divided by volume) and for all offset and break-even calculations. A model built on the gross NEAR RPC figure would overstate the effective capture rate by nearly 2x and produce a falsely optimistic break-even threshold.

On-Chain Evidence: What the Settlement Contract Shows

Decoding raw dip4 settlement events from intents.near (2,852 fills across 1,151 settlements, a contiguous window of Oct 28, 2026, ingestion ongoing) reveals something the aggregate data cannot: the settlement contract itself collects an explicit fee of roughly 0.01 bps per side, several hundred times below the 5.91 bps revenue rate implied by DefiLlama. Protocol revenue is therefore generated off the settlement contract, through application-layer fees or solver arrangements. The capture rate this model depends on is policy, not protocol: nothing at the contract level guarantees it, and it can change in either direction without governance. This is the strongest argument for the fee switch discussed in Section 8: moving the take into the contract converts a soft revenue arrangement into an enforced one.

The same events quantify the concentration risk flagged in Section 7. The top three solver accounts fill 86% of settled volume (Herfindahl index 0.33, 17 active solver accounts in the window). Concentration this high cuts both ways: it means a solver exit is a material volume event, and it means solvers hold real bargaining power against any attempt to raise the protocol take. Volume quality, however, screens clean: zero bilateral self-fills in the window, and non-solver round-trip activity of about 8%, consistent with organic flow rather than wash trading.

These figures come from a short window of one trading day and carry time-of-day bias; they are structure measurements, not averages. The ingestion pipeline continues to run, and every figure in this subsection regenerates from the raw events with one command as coverage grows.

4. HSP-029: The Governance Catalyst

HSP-029 was submitted to governance in Q4 2026 and activated on November 2, 2026. It mandates a linear reduction in the annual issuance cap from 2.5% → 1.6% over 24 months, stepping down by 0.9 percentage points evenly across the ramp. The 1.6% floor holds permanently once reached in November 2028.

What HSP-029 Changes

At the floor, daily NEAR issuance drops from 89,701 to 57,409 NEAR, a 36% reduction. The offset ratio improves from 19.2% to 30.0% at flat volume. The break-even volume requirement falls from $663M to $424M/day. Both figures assume the 5.91 bps capture rate holds; under the conservative 4.11 bps estimate the floor-scenario break-even is $610M/day.

What HSP-029 Does Not Change

The proposal does not touch the capture rate, the solver fee structure, or the protocol's ability to direct revenue to buybacks rather than operational costs. The fee-switch mechanism (activating a protocol-level take from solver fees, rather than relying on voluntary contribution) is a separate governance question and is the single lever that could most directly improve the offset ratio without requiring volume growth.

6. What Must Be True for Break-Even

Three conditions must hold simultaneously:

Volume growth of 3.3-5.2×. Intents volume needs to reach $424-663M/day. That is plausible if NEAR becomes the primary settlement layer for cross-chain DeFi volume from Ethereum, Solana, and Bitcoin, but it requires winning solver share in a competitive market where UniswapX, CoW Protocol, and 1inch are already established.

HSP-029 holding at the floor. The 24-month ramp is governance-enforced; a subsequent proposal could raise the cap again. The investment case is structurally weakened if issuance reverts upward before volume catches up.

Capture rate staying at or above 5.91 bps. The 4.11 bps estimate (90d trailing revenue/volume) represents the conservative case. If more of the fee revenue is absorbed by solvers through rebates or competitive pressure, the effective capture rate compresses and the break-even volume rises.

7. Key Risks

Volume concentration risk. If a small number of solvers or routes account for the majority of Intents volume, any disruption to that activity (a competing protocol, a solver exit, a bridge exploit) causes an outsized drop in buyback revenue.

Price sensitivity. The break-even formula is linear in NEAR price: a 50% NEAR price decline (to ~$2.19) requires 50% more volume to break even, all else equal. Conversely, a price rally to $8+ cuts the required volume in half.

HSP-029 reversal. Governance proposals can be overridden. A future proposal restoring the 2.5% cap would widen the gap back to the worst-case scenario.

8. The Fee-Switch Option

The most structurally important upgrade not yet priced into the model is a protocol-level fee switch: a governance-controlled mechanism that sets a minimum protocol take (analogous to Uniswap's fee switch or the Curve gauge weight system). Under the current model, the protocol receives whatever solvers contribute voluntarily. A fee switch at 5 bps on matched Intents volume would double the effective capture rate, cutting break-even volume in half, from $479M to ~$240M/day under the HSP-029 floor scenario. That target is within reach of a single large routing deal or an Ethereum L2 integration.

Verdict · November 2, 2026
NEAR Intents is a genuine economic engine for NEAR, but at current scale it offsets 19 cents of issuance per dollar created. HSP-029 raises that ceiling to 30 cents over two years, meaningful progress but structurally insufficient without volume growth. The investment thesis becomes sound when two of the three following conditions are met: (1) Intents daily volume crosses $450M, (2) the fee switch activates at ≥5 bps, or (3) NEAR price sustains above $8. The fee-switch decision is the single highest-conviction near-term catalyst because it is in governance's control and volume-independent. Until then, HSP-029 is a necessary but not sufficient step toward a self-sustaining buyback loop.

9. Quantitative Model Limitations

Monte Carlo Volume Forecast

Probabilities span 17-99% across six scenarios because each method captures a different slice of history. No single number is the "answer." The range is the answer.

  • Student-t degrees-of-freedom fit by MLE at ν=2.66: with ν<3, variance is theoretically infinite. Even the fat-tail GBM underestimates the most extreme events.
  • GBM variants assume volume is independent of NEAR token price. If price and volume are correlated (both driven by macro sentiment), GBM path variance is understated.
  • Block-bootstrap preserves historical autocorrelation but cannot model structural breaks: a protocol exploit, regulatory ban, or competitor routing the majority of flow off-chain.
  • Break-even thresholds are fixed at the $4.37 spot price at model run date. The interactive dashboard lets you explore price sensitivity directly.
  • GBM 12/24/36-month probabilities are numerically equal because the process either crosses the threshold early in the path or drifts permanently below; not a model error.
Falsification threshold: if the realised 7-day average volume stays below $60M for 60 or more consecutive days, less than half today's $125M, the 90d base-case scenario is rejected. The 30d pessimistic scenario becomes operative.

Fee-Switch Difference-in-Differences

ATT = 1.10× (95% CI: 0.97-1.25×). The confidence interval includes 1.0: no statistically significant volume impact is detected relative to the three controls.

  • Four units total (NEAR + 3 controls): the permutation distribution has only four distinct points. The 75th-percentile rank is a weak signal, not a statistically powerful one.
  • Controls (1inch, Paraswap, Jupiter) are larger, more mature aggregators with different liquidity depth and routing characteristics.
  • The 90-day window coincided with a broader DeFi volume contraction; the market cycle is not isolated from the treatment effect.
  • Pre-period slopes differ: NEAR's pre-trend was +0.003/day while controls averaged -0.002/day. This suggests the parallel trends assumption is imperfect; the DiD estimate may conflate trend with treatment.
  • Bootstrap CIs are constructed by resampling within pre/post periods independently. Within-period autocorrelation is not preserved, which may narrow the stated intervals.
Interpretation: the fee switch did not detectably harm volume relative to peers. This is consistent with either (a) no harm or (b) insufficient statistical power to detect a modest effect at this sample size.
